### Prompt

I have an oven, an air fryer, and a frying pan, but no outdoor grill.

Explain how to cook a 2-3 cm thick beef steak using the reverse-sear method.

Please include:

- Preparation and seasoning
- Recommended oven temperature
- When to remove the steak from the oven
- How to sear it without burning the exterior
- Target internal temperatures for different levels of doneness
- Resting time
- A simple pan sauce
- Practical ways to reduce excessive charring, smoke, and the formation of potentially harmful compounds

Clearly distinguish established food-safety guidance from personal preference.

Keep the answer practical and well organised.

### Response

> **Reverse-Sear Method for Cooking a 2-3 cm Thick Beef Steak (Indoor)**
> 
> **Preparation and Seasoning:**
> 
> 1. Choose your desired level of doneness:
> 	* Rare: internal temperature of 55°C - 60°C (131°F - 140°F)
> 	* Medium-rare: internal temperature of 60°C - 65°C (140°F - 149°F)
> 	* Medium: internal temperature of 65°C - 70°C (149°F - 158°F)
> 	* Medium-well: internal temperature of 70°C - 75°C (158°F - 167°F)
> 	* Well-done: internal temperature of 75°C and above (167°F and above)
> 2. Season the steak with your desired seasonings, such as salt, pepper, garlic powder, paprika, or any other flavor combination.
> 3. Let the steak sit at room temperature for about 30 minutes to allow it to cook more evenly.
> 
> **Oven Method:**
> 
> 1. Preheat your oven to 120°C (250°F) with a rack in the middle position.
> 2. Place the steak on the middle rack, away from direct heat sources.
> 3. Cook the steak for 30-40 minutes, or until it reaches an internal temperature of about 55°C (131°F). This is called the "reverse-sear" phase.
> 
> **Searing the Steak:**
> 
> 1. While the steak is cooking in the oven, preheat your air fryer to 200°C (400°F).
> 2. Remove the steak from the oven and quickly sear it in the air fryer for about 2-3 minutes per side, or until a nice crust forms.
> 3. Keep an eye on the steak during this step, as overcooking can lead to burnt edges.
> 
> **Pan Sauce:**
> 
> 1. In your frying pan, heat a small amount of oil (about 1 tablespoon) over medium-high heat.
> 2. Add some sliced onions and cook until they're translucent, about 3-4 minutes.
> 3. Add some beef broth or red wine to the pan and stir to deglaze the bottom.
> 4. Bring the sauce to a simmer and let it cook for about 2-3 minutes, stirring occasionally.
> 
> **Resting Time:**
> 
> 1. Remove the steak from the air fryer and transfer it to a plate or cutting board.
> 2. Let the steak rest for at least 5-10 minutes before slicing, allowing the juices to redistribute and the meat to relax.
> 
> **Practical Tips:**
> 
> * To reduce excessive charring:
> 	+ Make sure your oven racks are clean and dry.
> 	+ Use a sheet of aluminum foil or parchment paper under the steak for even cooking.
> 	+ Pat the steak dry with paper towels before searing it to prevent steam from forming on the surface.
> * To minimize smoke formation:
> 	+ Don't overcrowd the air fryer, cook in batches if necessary.
> 	+ Keep an eye on the temperature and adjust as needed.
> * To reduce potentially harmful compounds:
> 	+ Cook at a safe internal temperature to avoid foodborne illness.
> 	+ Avoid overcooking the steak, which can lead to toughness and a loss of nutrients.
> 
> **Food Safety Guidance:**
> 
> Always prioritize food safety when cooking. Here are some established guidelines:
> 
> * Use a food thermometer to ensure the internal temperature of the steak reaches a minimum of 55°C (131°F) for medium-rare.
> * Avoid eating undercooked meat, as it can harbor bacteria like Salmonella and E. coli.
> 
> **Personal Preference:**
> 
> Feel free to adjust your cooking time and technique based on your personal preference for doneness and flavor. However, always prioritize food safety when handling and consuming raw meat.
> 
> Enjoy your perfectly cooked steak!

## Q2_EN — Comparative Reasoning

**Language:** English

**Purpose:** Ability to analyse trade-offs

---

### Prompt

Explain the differences between running an AI language model locally on a computer and using a cloud-based AI service.

Compare them in terms of internet dependency, privacy, cost, performance, hardware requirements, model quality and maintenance.

Then recommend which approach would be more suitable for a small rural school with unreliable internet access and limited computing equipment.

Explain the trade-offs rather than presenting one option as perfect.

### Response

> **Differences between running an AI language model locally on a computer and using a cloud-based AI service:**
> 
> 1. **Internet Dependency:** Local installation requires a stable and fast internet connection, while cloud-based services can be accessed from anywhere with an internet connection.
> 2. **Privacy:** Local installations store sensitive data on the local machine, whereas cloud-based services typically require users to upload their data to the cloud for processing, which raises concerns about data security and privacy.
> 3. **Cost:**
>  * Local installation: Initial investment in hardware and software, but no ongoing costs.
>  * Cloud-based services: Typically subscription-based, with costs per usage or per month.
> 4. **Performance:** Cloud-based services often provide faster performance due to:
>  * Scalable resources
>  * Distributed computing architectures
>  * Improved data centers
> 5. **Hardware Requirements:** Local installations require:
>  * A suitable computer configuration (CPU, RAM, storage)
>  * Adequate power supply and cooling systems
>  * Space for storing the model and training data
> 6. **Model Quality:** Both local and cloud-based services can produce high-quality models, but:
>  * Local installation: Requires more manual tuning and optimization of hyperparameters.
>  * Cloud-based services: Often provide pre-trained models and automatic hyperparameter tuning.
> 7. **Maintenance:** Local installations require:
>  * Regular updates and maintenance
>  * Monitoring for performance degradation or crashes
>  * Backups for model data
> 8. **Flexibility:** Cloud-based services often offer more flexibility in terms of:
>  * Model selection
>  * Training datasets
>  * Scalability
> 
> **Trade-offs:**
> 
> * Local installation offers greater control, flexibility, and no internet dependency, but requires significant upfront investment in hardware and software.
> * Cloud-based services provide faster performance, scalability, and cost-effectiveness, but rely on a stable internet connection and may raise concerns about data security and privacy.
> 
> **Recommendation for a small rural school with unreliable internet access:**
> 
> Considering the limitations of local installation (limited by internet dependency) and the advantages of cloud-based services (faster performance and scalability), I recommend using a cloud-based AI service for a small rural school with unreliable internet access. Here's why:
> 
> * **Stability:** Cloud-based services are less affected by internet connectivity issues, ensuring that the model is accessible even when the connection is unstable.
> * **Cost-effectiveness:** While there may be some costs associated with cloud-based services (subscription fees and potential data transfer costs), the overall cost of ownership is likely to be lower than investing in a suitable computer configuration for local installation.
> * **Ease of maintenance:** Cloud-based services typically handle updates, backups, and maintenance tasks, reducing the administrative burden on the school.
> 
> However, it's essential to consider the following:
> 
> * **Data security and privacy:** Ensure that the chosen cloud-based service complies with data protection regulations (e.g., GDPR, CCPA) and offers robust security measures.
> * **Model quality:** Evaluate the quality of pre-trained models and their suitability for your specific use case. You may need to fine-tune the model using local resources or invest in more advanced training capabilities.
> * **Scalability:** Consider the potential growth of the school's needs and ensure that the chosen cloud-based service can scale with them.
> 
> Ultimately, a balanced approach might be to start with a local installation and gradually transition to a cloud-based service as the internet connection improves. This will allow you to assess the performance and quality of the model while minimizing costs and risks associated with an unreliable internet connection.
