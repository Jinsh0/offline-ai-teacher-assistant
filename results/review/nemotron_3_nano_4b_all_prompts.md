### Prompt

I have an oven, an air fryer, and a frying pan, but no outdoor grill.

Explain how to cook a 2-3 cm thick beef steak using the reverse-sear method.

Please include:

- Preparation and seasoning
- Recommended oven temperature
- When to remove the steak from the oven
- How to sear it without burning the exterior
- Target internal temperatures for different levels of doneness
- Resting time
- A simple pan sauce
- Practical ways to reduce excessive charring, smoke, and the formation of potentially harmful compounds

Clearly distinguish established food-safety guidance from personal preference.

Keep the answer practical and well organised.

### Response

### Cooking a 2–3 cm (¾‑inch) Beef Steak by Reverse‑Sear  
*(All times are approximate; adjust for thickness, cut of meat, or equipment.)*

---

## 1. Overview & Safety First  
| Step | Food‑Safety / Established Guidance | Personal Preference |
|------|-----------------------------------|----------------------|
| **Start** (prep) | Heat steak to ≥ 74 °C (165 °F) minimum for safety; this will happen in the oven. | Use a temperature you like, e.g., medium‑rare ~55 °C / 130 °F. |
| **Oven finish** | Oven gives an even heat zone; you control the final internal temp with a probe. | You may want more or less carryover after rest. |
| **Resting** | Resting allows juices to re‑distribute but does not change safety – steak stays ≥ 74 °C if already reached. | Extending rest yields juicier texture (your preference). |

---

## 2. Preparation & Seasoning  

1. **Take a good cut**: 2–3 cm thick, well‑marbled (rib‑eye, skirt, or eye‑spot steak). Trim any silver skin.  
2. **Pat dry** with paper towels – moisture reduces browning and promotes steaming rather than searing.  

### Salt & Fat  
* Coat the steak on both sides with **fine sea salt** (≈ ½–¾ tsp per side). Let it sit 15‑30 min at room temperature; this draws out surface moisture, then re‑absorbs for better crust.  

### Optional Boosts (still safe)  
* Drizzle **1–2 tbsp high‑smoke‑point oil** (grapeseed, avocado, refined coconut) with the salt; it protects proteins from early charring and helps Maillard reaction.  
* Add a **dry rub** – e.g., ¼ tsp cracked black pepper, ⅛ tsp garlic powder, a pinch of smoked paprika (adds flavor, not colour). No sugar unless you want quick caramelisation; the reverse‑sear avoids that risk.  

---

## 3. Oven Settings & Resting Time to Remove from Oven  

| Target Doneness | Approx. Internal Temp (°C/°F) | Oven Temp* (convection on low, non‑convection oven) | Time Before Removing |
|-----------------|------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|----------------------|
| Rare            | 43–45 °C / 109–113 °F        | **200 °C** (incl. steam) or **180 °C** (convection)   | When steak hits ~45 °C |
| Medium‑rare     | 49–51 °C / 120–124 °F        | Same as above                                        | When steak reaches 50 °C |
| Medium          | 57–60 °C / 135–140 °F       | Same                                                 | When ~58 °C |
| Medium‑well      | 63 °C / 145 °F               | Same                                                 | When ~65 °C |
| Well‑done        | ≥ 71 °C / 160 °F             | Same (if you need to push higher)                  | As above |

\* **Why this range?** Even ovens with the most accurate probes still have a ±5–8 °C variance. Starting at 200 °C/392 °F guarantees heat penetrates quickly; the actual reading is lower once meat stops cooking.

**When to pull:** Use an instant‑read probe inserted through the thickest, least fat portion (through the top surface). When the tip reads your desired range **and you hold it steady for ~15 seconds**, remove immediately. Oven residual heat will raise temp slightly during rest.

---

## 4. Searing Steps — How to Get a Crisp Face Without Burning  

| Issue | Why It Happens | Fix (Equipment + Technique) |
|-------|----------------|-----------------------------|
| **Excessive char** | Too much oil, high flame, contact for > 30 s | Use a **non‑stick or cast iron pan**; heat **medium‑high** → swirl until it just begins to smoke (≈ 250 °C). Add steak; stir gently for ≤ 30 s per side. |
| **Searing without burning** | Pan not hot enough, meat sits too long | Apply a **thin film of oil**; when the pan reaches 250–270 °C (visible shimmer), place steak in one layer; you’ll get a rapid crust before proteins denature. |
| **Burnt flavor compounds (acrylamide)** | Oven temperature spikes, meat left too long over high heat | Keep oven at max 200 °C; avoid “standing” the steak > 45 s after it reaches ~45°C in oven. The reverse‑sear leaves only a brief sear phase. |

**Searing sequence**  
1. Place steak skin‑side down (if it has a fat cap) or the side with less seasoning on **down**. Press gently against pan to release the meat and let excess oil drip away.  
2. Let it sit for **≈ 45–60 seconds** until crust is golden‑brown, not blackened.  
3. Flip with tongs; sear **another 30–45 seconds**.  
4. Remove to plate immediately.

---

## 5. Target Internal Temperatures (Safety & Desired Texture)  

| Doneness | Temp °C / °F | Recommended Rest |
|----------|--------------|-------------------|
| Rare (undercooked but safe if > 165°c in core is not required for thin steak) – **avoid** under 45 °C. | 43–45 °C / 109–113 °F | 5‑7 min |
| Medium‑rare (ideal) | 55 °C / 131 °F | 8‑12 min |
| Medium | 60–62 °C / 140–145 °F | 10‑15 min |
| Well done (≥ 71 °C) | ≥ 71 °C / 160 °F | 5 min (rest shortens collagen breakdown) |

**Key point:** Every 2–3 cm of thickness adds ~9 °C rise after cooking. A 2‑cm steak can reach 55 °C at **≈ 44 °C oven temp for ~70 min**, well under the target.

---

## 6. Resting the Steak  

1. Transfer seared steak to a warm plate (or pre‑warmed plate in a pan).  
2. Tent loosely with foil or parchment – this retains heat but prevents soggy crust.  
3. **Rest time**:  
   * **Rare / medium‑rare** : 10 min (allows juices to redistribute).  
   * **Medium & beyond** : 8–12 min.  

*This step does not make the steak unsafe; it’s a quality enhancement.*

---

## 7. Simple Pan Sauce – “Beef Melt”  

**Ingredients (serves 4)**  
- ¼ cup butter, cut into cubes  
- 2–3 garlic cloves, minced  
- 1 tsp sherry or dry white wine, reduced (≈ ½ tsp)  
- A splash of beef broth (2 tbsp) – optional for extra flavor  

**Method**  
1. In the hot pan (after steak is removed), **reduce heat to low**. Melt 3 butter cubes.  
2. Add minced garlic; sauté ≤ 30 s until fragrant (do not darken).  
3. Deglaze with wine, let bubble 15 s.  
4. Stir in remaining butter (softens it into sauce); add broth if you like a thinner consistency.  

**Result:** A glossy, aromatic beurre rouge that complements the steak without drowning flavor.

---

## 8. Practical Tips to Reduce Excessive Charring & Harmful Compounds  

| Problem | Root Cause | Mitigation |
|---------|------------|------------|
| **Char from oven** | Too high temperature, or meat retained too long in hot environment | Keep oven ≤ 200 °C; remove steak as soon as probe hits target. Use a thin layer of oil that evaporates quickly (no pool). |
| **Searing smoke / acrylamide formation** | High‑heat pan contact > 30 s, especially after ~160 °C | Preheat only until it *shimmers*; add steak in one piece; limit contact time to < 45 s/side. Use a stainless steel or cast iron pan that retains heat uniformly. |
| **Over‑cooking → dryness / increased acrylamide risk** (acrylamide forms when meat is heated > 120 °C for 30 min+) | Long oven dwell at high temp | Oven time < 45 min; use thermometer; stop at the temperature you intended. |
| **Uneven crust, dry spots** | Thickness variation or pan temperature lag | Verify steak thickness uniformly 2‑3 cm; if one spot is thicker, consider slicing opposite way next meal (fat cap down). |

---

## 9. Quick Reference Checklist  

| Step | Action | Time / Temp |
|------|--------|-------------|
| **Prep** | Salt steak → drip light oil → dry | — |
| **Oven** | Insert probe, place steak | 200 °C (incl. steam) |
| **Pull** | Probe ≥ 45 °C (rare), ≤ 58 °C (medium‑well) | Pull at exact reading |
| **Sear** | Medium‑high pan, ~270 °C | ≤ 60 s/side |
| **Rest** | Tent foil → 10 min (rare/med) / 12 min (well) | — |
| **Sauce** | Sweat garlic in butter + wine | Done while resting |

---

### Bottom Line  
- The reverse‑sear guarantees **safety** (≥ 74 °C internal temperature before you start the brief sear).  
- All technical recommendations above are based on **established food‑safety ranges**.  
- Your personal taste (e.g., preferring a darker crust or longer resting) is separate and should be applied after confirming the meat reaches the target heat.  

Happy steaking! 🍽️

## Q2_EN — Comparative Reasoning

**Language:** English

**Purpose:** Ability to analyse trade-offs

### Prompt

Explain the differences between running an AI language model locally on a computer and using a cloud-based AI service.

Compare them in terms of internet dependency, privacy, cost, performance, hardware requirements, model quality and maintenance.

Then recommend which approach would be more suitable for a small rural school with unreliable internet access and limited computing equipment.

Explain the trade-offs rather than presenting one option as perfect.

### Response

Running an AI language model locally on a computer or using cloud-based AI services each have distinct advantages and limitations, especially in context like a **small rural school** with **unreliable internet access** and **limited computing equipment**. Below is a comparison across key factors:

---

### 1. **Internet Dependency**
- **Local (on-premise)**: ✅ Independent of connectivity. Runs entirely offline once the model is loaded.
- **Cloud-based**: ❌ Requires reliable, high-speed internet to access APIs, upload data, and receive responses.

> *In a rural setting with unstable internet*, cloud services become impractical due to frequent disconnections or slow transmission.

---

### 2. **Privacy & Data Sensitivity**
- **Local**: ✅ High privacy. All processing happens on the device; no data leaves the school.
- **Cloud-based**: ❌ Lower confidentiality. Text input may be stored or transmitted by third-party providers (e.g., AWS, Azure). Sensitive student information could pose risks.

> *Students’ questions about grades, social issues, or sensitive topics become safer with local models*, as there's no external data trail.

---

### 3. **Cost**
- **Local**: 💰 Low immediate cost after initial setup (no monthly fees), but high upfront hardware and electricity demands.
- **Cloud-based**: 💶 Ongoing operational cost based on usage (e.g., per query or hour). Easier to integrate for small-scale use.

> *Despite lower up-front cost*, cloud services may eventually exceed it during a school’s lifetime, especially with frequent use.

---

### 4. **Performance**
- **Local**: ⚠️ Limited by hardware (CPU/GPU). Smaller models run slower; large models require powerful infrastructure.
- **Cloud-based**: ✅ Can scale performance instantly with provider infrastructure—faster responses and larger models.

> *However*, in schools without good computers or power backup, local systems may crash during school hours due to overheating or slow startup times.

---

### 5. **Hardware Requirements**
- **Local**: 🖥️ High initial investment: powerful GPU, sufficient RAM (8–32GB+), fast storage. Cooling and setup expertise needed.
- **Cloud-based**: ☁️ Minimal hardware—just a web browser or device.

> *Rural schools often lack access to such equipment*, making local deployment difficult without significant funding.

---

### 6. **Model Quality**
- **Local**: ✅ Can use latest models locally (e.g., fine-tuned versions), avoiding lag or reliance on vendor updates.
- **Cloud-based**: ⚠️ Limited to what the supplier offers; quality may degrade over time due to policy changes or cost-cutting.

> *Fine-tuned local models* can be tailored to educational needs better than generic cloud chatbots.

---

### 7. **Maintenance**
- **Local**: 🔧 Requires technical knowledge: updates, bug fixes, hardware repair.
- **Cloud-based**: 👥 Handled by the provider—zero maintenance needed.

> *But a broken device is easier to fix locally than troubleshoot intermittent cloud outages.*

---

### Summary Trade-Offs in Practice

| Factor | Local AI (Pros/Cons) | Cloud AI (Pros/Cons) |
|-------|----------------------|------------------------|
| **Internet** | Offline, reliable—✅ / ❌ high internet needed. | Constant connectivity—✅ low requirement. / ❌ likely unstable in rural areas. |
| **Privacy** | Fully within school control—✅ / ❌ none. | Data outside school jurisdiction—❌ sensitive info risk. |
| **Cost** | High upfront, low long-term—✅ / ❌ expensive hardware. | Low setup, variable use-based cost—✅ / ❌ can grow with usage. |
| **Performance** | Slow, limited by device—⚠️ / ✅ depends on tech. | Fast and stable—✅ / ❌ outage-prone. |
| **Hardware** | Needs powerful PC/GPU—⚠️ expensive/electronic fragility. | Minimal hardware—✅ but limited to device capabilities. |
| **Model Quality** | Customizable, up-to-date—✅ / ❌ may lag. | Depends on vendor—⚠️ fixed or costly to change. |
| **Maintenance** | Needs tech/IT support—⚠️ risky without staff. | No maintenance—✅ but vendor liable for failure. |

---

### Recommendation: **A Hybrid or Local-First Approach**

Given the context, a small rural school should prioritize a **local implementation using lightweight AI models**, even if it requires significant effort and investment.

#### Why?
- Internet is unreliable → cloud fails.
- Privacy concerns with student data → avoid third-party exposure.
- Limited hardware → high upfront cost is daunting but manageable once.

#### Suggestions:
1. **Start Simple**: Use a small, offline model (e.g., GPT4All or Llama 3 8B) installed on a laptop or Raspberry Pi, if possible.
2. **Use Cloud Only for Non-Sensitive Tasks**: E.g., public Q&A tools that don’t store personal data.
3. **Explore Edge AI**: Use locally hosted AI with voice input via headset (no full chat needed).
4. **Phase Budget Developmentally**: Seek grants or use surplus university tech.

#### Trade-Offs Remained
- A local system may feel slower than cloud tools—acceptable trade-off for stability.
- Hardware is heavier upfront, but a single well-chosen device could last years.
- Maintenance requires staff training—but one person can learn over time.

---

### Conclusion

**For a small rural school**:  
> **Local AI modeling offers the best long-term sustainability and privacy protection**, despite higher initial costs and technical demands.  
> Cloud services shine in schools with stable internet and disposable resources, but are risky here due to dependency on an unreliable network.

The ideal path is often **local-first experimentation**—keeping education tools under school control while minimizing exposure to real-world digital vulnerabilities.

## Q2_ID — Comparative Reasoning

**Language:** Bahasa Indonesia

**Purpose:** Ability to analyse trade-offs

### Prompt

Jelaskan perbedaan antara menjalankan model AI secara lokal di komputer sendiri dengan menggunakan layanan AI berbasis cloud.

Bandingkan keduanya dari sisi ketergantungan terhadap koneksi internet, privasi, biaya, performa, kebutuhan perangkat keras, kualitas model, dan pemeliharaan.

Kemudian, berikan rekomendasi mengenai pendekatan mana yang lebih cocok untuk sekolah kecil di daerah pedesaan dengan akses internet yang tidak stabil dan perangkat komputer yang terbatas.

Jelaskan pro dan kontra masing-masing pendekatan, dan jangan menggambarkan salah satu pilihan sebagai solusi yang sempurna.

### Response

**Perbedaan antara menjalankan model AI secara lokal di komputer sendiri dengan menggunakan layanan AI berbasis cloud**

**1. Ketergantungan terhadap koneksi internet:**
- **Lokal (on-premise):** Tidak memerlukan ketahanan internet; model bisa digunakan 24/7 independent dari kestabilitas jaringan.
- **Cloud:** Memerlukan koneksi internet stabil untuk mengirim data ke server dan menerima respons. Jika internet rusak, layanan AI hilang.

**2. Privasi:**
- **Lokal:** Akuntasi ke dalam mesin local; akses hanya internasional per komputer. Semua data berada di device yang digunakan.
- **Cloud:** Data (seperti gambar, teks, atau input pengguna) dialihkan ke server pihak ketiga, sehingga risiko keterikagunaan lebih tinggi.

**3. Biaya:**
- **Lokal:** Inisial biaya tinggi (perpustakaan perangkat keras, pengembangan software, perbaikan); biaya operasional sangat rendah.
- **Cloud:** Modal berupa paket atau pay-as-you-go; biaya tetap terhitung dari penggunaan data dan waktu proses.

**4. Punyaforma:**
- **Lokal:** Performa dipengaruhi oleh spesifikasi komputer (CPU, RAM). Jika perangkatnya lambat, AI bisa berat.
- **Cloud:** Proses umumnya cepat karena di server dengan spesifikasi tinggi; tidak terlimit keputungan perangkat.

**5. Kekayaan model:**
- **Lokal:** Models besar (seperti LLM) bisa tak bisa dimampu ke dalam satu komputer kecil dan memakan RAM/SSD.
- **Cloud:** Model besar sangat umum digunakan (seperti GPT, Gemini), karena dikelola oleh penyedia AI dengan sumber daya sufficient.

**6. Pemeliharaan:**
- **Lokal:** Perusahaan harus menjalankan updates model, backup data, dan perbaikan keamanan – tabaian teknis dan biaya time.
- **Cloud:** Resmi di dalam provider (seperti Google Cloud, AWS). Provider memantau keamanan, update, dan support 24/7. Akuis dari sistem.

---

**Rekomendasi untuk sekolah kecil di daerah pedesaan:**

Pendekatan yang lebih cocok: **Lokal** atau kombinasi dari lokal dengan sumber daya terjangkau (misalnya model kecil).

Ketika internet tidak stable dan perangkat terbatas, pendekatan cloud bisa menguras biaya operasional dan keamanan data karena menyalakan layanan.

Dengan ini, **rekomendasi** adalah menggunakan **model AI lokal dengan penggalian ringan**, misalnya:

- Model kecil (Quantized, TinyML) yang memangkannya RAM dan CPU.
- Server atau PC pribadi sebagai server local untuk semua kebutuhan AI.
- Memiliki one-click atau otomatisasi penyalin model dari platform stabil (seperti TensorFlow Lite, ONNX).

| Aspek           | Lokal                              | Cloud                              |

We need to find:
1. How many students are in *at least* one extracurricular activity?

### 1. How many students participate in **at least** one extracurricular activity?

We are told:

$$
\frac{3}{5} \times 240
$$

   $$

   $$

   $$

   $$

$$

25% means the same as $ \frac{25}{100} = 0.25 $ (or just 1/4).

So:
$$
\frac{1}{4} \times 144 = ?
$$

Divide 144 by 4:
$$
144 \div 4 = 36
$$

✅ So, **36 students** participate in the science club.

---

### ✅ Final Answers:

1. **144 students** participate in at least one extracurricular activity.
2. **36 of those students** are also in the science club.

   \[
